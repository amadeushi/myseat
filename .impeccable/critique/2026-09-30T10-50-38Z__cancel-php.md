## Overall Impression

This is a genuinely well-crafted transactional page — warm copy, real accessibility wins (`dl/dt/dd`, `aria-labelledby`, native `<details>`), and color tokens that hold up under actual WCAG math (every checked text/background pair clears AA, several clear AAA). The single biggest opportunity is that the two things a guest most needs at a moment of friction — a safety net to back out of cancelling, and calm rather than alarm at first glance — are exactly where the page currently falls short: the "keep it" button was written in copy and never shipped, and a red exit sign sits above the reassuring facts on every visit, not just the ones where the guest actually wants to cancel.

## What's Working

1. **The cancellation copy reframes the ask, not just states it.** "So someone else can take your seat" is a specific, non-generic emotional choice that fits the "gilded service handbook" voice — most systems would just say "confirm cancellation."
2. **Accessible markup without a framework doing the work.** Semantic `dl/dt/dd` fact pairs, `aria-labelledby` on every section, native `<details>/<summary>` for disclosure instead of custom JS accordions — all correct, unprompted choices.
3. **The color contrast discipline actually measures out.** Every token pair Assessment B computed clears WCAG AA (muted text on surface: 6.56–7.00:1; danger-red: 6.56–7.00:1; gold: 7.27–10.65:1) — this wasn't asserted, it was measured pixel-value to pixel-value and holds.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] Missing "keep my reservation" safety control**
Why it matters: This is the single destructive action in the entire guest journey. `$t['keep']` ("No, keep my reservation") and `$t['confirm_title']` are fully translated in both languages (cancel.php lines 45-48, 81-84) but never rendered — inside the confirm `<details>` (lines 322-332), only the destructive `.guest-danger` button exists. The safety net was designed and then dropped in implementation.
Fix: Add a secondary button/link using `$t['keep']` next to `.guest-danger`, styled like the existing `.confirm-secondary` class so it doesn't visually compete with the danger action.
Suggested command: `/impeccable harden`

**[P1] Several touch targets fail the 44×44px floor on a page reached almost entirely by phone**
Why it matters: `.lang-cancel` (the language-switcher's X) measures 26×26px, `.lang-picker a` (EN/DE) measures 36×21 / 35×21px, and `.confirm-secondary` ("Back to website," used on the lookup/done/notfound states) is a bare 378×15px text link with no padding. This page is overwhelmingly opened from an email link on a phone — these are exactly the taps most likely to mis-hit.
Fix: Give `.lang-cancel` and `.lang-picker a` real min-height/min-width to 44px (padding, not just font-size), and pad `.confirm-secondary` into a proper tap target rather than a bare inline text link.
Suggested command: `/impeccable adapt`

**[P1] Cancel-page form inputs are 1px under the design system's own "16px Form Rule"**
Why it matters: `.wizard-step input[type="text"]` (used by both the `nr` and `email` lookup fields) is set to `font-size: 15px`. DESIGN.md's own named rule exists specifically to prevent iOS Safari's auto-zoom-on-focus — and this form is reached almost exclusively on mobile.
Fix: Bump `.wizard-step input[type="text"]`, `textarea`, `select` to 16px.
Suggested command: `/impeccable typeset`

**[P2] The one non-gold accent color on the page leads with an exit sign, not a welcome**
Why it matters: `.guest-jump` ("Cancel reservation," colored `--danger`) sits immediately below the welcoming lead paragraph, before the guest has seen a single fact about their own booking. On the majority of visits — where the guest is just confirming details, not cancelling — this front-loads an anxious, exit-colored cue ahead of the reassurance the page is otherwise built to deliver.
Fix: Move the jump link below `.guest-facts`, or render it in `--text-muted` at that position, reserving `--danger` for inside `.guest-cancel-box` itself.
Suggested command: `/impeccable quieter`

**[P3] The cancel jump-link doesn't finish what it promises, and there's no landmark structure**
Why it matters: `.guest-jump` targets `#g-cancel`, an id on an `<h2>` that sits above a still-*closed* `<details>` — activating it scrolls sighted users to the heading, but the actual cancel control is one more, un-signposted click away inside a collapsed disclosure. There's also no `<main>` landmark anywhere on the page, so keyboard/screen-reader users have nothing to jump into on load.
Fix: Wrap page content in `<main>`; either point the jump link at the `<summary>` itself, or script `open` onto `.guest-cancel-box` when the jump link is used.
Suggested command: `/impeccable harden`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (Confused First-Timer, arrived via the emailed token link)**: The first colored thing Jordan sees below the welcome headline is a red "Cancel reservation" link — for someone just checking their reservation, this reads as "this page is mainly about cancelling." If Jordan *does* click it, the button isn't actually there yet (still-closed accordion, P3) — Jordan may conclude the link is broken. If Jordan opens the accordion and changes their mind, there's no "No, keep it" button to click (P0) — the only way out is realizing they can re-collapse a native disclosure widget, which isn't an obvious affordance to a non-technical user.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent, keyboard/screen-reader user)**: The semantic wins are real — `dl/dt/dd`, `aria-labelledby`, native `<details>` all announce and operate correctly. But Sam has no `<main>` landmark to jump into and must tab or read through the entire brand row and language nav first. Activating the `#g-cancel` jump link lands Sam on a non-focusable `<h2>`, one section short of the actual actionable control, with no announced hint that a second activation is needed.

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User, opened the email on her phone)**: Tapping either lookup-form field triggers Mobile Safari's auto-zoom (15px inputs, P1) — a disorienting zoom exactly when Casey is trying to move fast. If she wants to cancel one-handed: tap jump link → notice still-closed accordion → tap summary → read confirm text → tap danger button, a four-tap chain with zero progress feedback on a possibly spotty connection, and no guard against a double-tap double-submitting the cancellation.

## Minor Observations

1. The signed cancel token (`t=`) is re-embedded in the "Add to calendar" download URL — not a design defect, but a second surface carrying the same credential, worth a security-adjacent glance.
2. The booking-number field is validated server-side against `^[A-Za-z0-9]{1,12}$` but nothing in the UI hints at the expected format, so a pasted number with spaces/dashes just produces a generic "not found."
3. `.guest-danger` has no `disabled`/`.is-saving` treatment, unlike `.submit-button` elsewhere in the sheet — no guard against an impatient double-tap double-submitting on a slow connection.
4. "Add to calendar" (`.guest-ics`) shares the exact same `.guest-btn` styling as the menu-link buttons below it — no visual signal that it's a distinct utility action rather than page content.
5. The `notfound` message bundles three possible causes (wrong number, wrong email/phone, already cancelled) into one undifferentiated paragraph with no per-field guidance.

## Questions to Consider
